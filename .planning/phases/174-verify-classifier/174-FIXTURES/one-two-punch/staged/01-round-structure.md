# Starting a New Round — Plan and Fight phases (p.1, continues on p.2)

> Note: this section begins on p.1 and continues onto p.2, where its third phase
> ("3) DISCARD") is defined. See `02-punch-examples-discard.md`.

p.1, Starting a New Round:
Each round has three phases: Plan, Fight, and Discard.
Both you and your opponent must complete each phase at the same time.

p.1, 1) PLAN:
Choose any two action cards from your hand and place them in any order face-down next to your boxer. The card closest to your boxer is your first action and the other card is your second action. Both you and your opponent have the same set of cards and may look through each other's discard pile at any time.

p.1, 2) FIGHT:
You and your opponent must flip your first action card face up at the same time and compare their timings to determine the order they are resolved.
The player with the lower timing on their card must resolve their action first. If the timing is the same on both cards, they are resolved at the same time.

p.1, 2) FIGHT (example, italic):
For example, if Red played a Jab and Blue played a Retreat, Red would resolve their Jab first since it has a timing of 1 and Retreat has a timing of 2.

Derived (p.1): A round is a fixed sequence of three phases — Plan, then Fight, then Discard — and both players act simultaneously within each phase.
Derived (p.1): Each player commits exactly two action cards per round, face down, with a fixed order (first action nearest the boxer, second action farther away).
Derived (p.1): A player must therefore hold at least two action cards in hand to plan a round.
Derived (p.1): Card timings are numeric; lower timing resolves earlier. Equal timings resolve simultaneously.
Derived (p.1): Jab has timing 1 and Retreat has timing 2 (stated in the worked example).
Derived (p.1): Both players have identical action-card sets (mirrored by color), so the hidden information is only which cards each has played or discarded — discard piles are open information to both players at all times.
