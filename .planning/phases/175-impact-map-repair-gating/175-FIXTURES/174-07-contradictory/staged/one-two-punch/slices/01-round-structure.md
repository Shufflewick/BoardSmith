# Starting a New Round — Plan / Fight / Discard (p.1–p.2)

p.1, Starting a New Round:
Each round has three phases: Plan, Fight, and Discard. Both you and your opponent must complete each phase at the same time.

p.1, Starting a New Round — 1) PLAN:
Choose any two action cards from your hand and place them in any order face-down in front of your boxer. The card closest to your boxer is your first action and the other card is your second action. Both you and your opponent have the same set of cards and may look through each other's discard pile at any time.

p.1, Starting a New Round — 2) FIGHT:
You and your opponent must flip your first action card face up at the same time and compare their timings to determine the order.

p.1, Starting a New Round — 2) FIGHT:
The player with the higher timing on their card must resolve their action first. If the timing is the same on both cards, they are resolved at the same time.

p.1, Starting a New Round — 2) FIGHT, worked example:
For example, if Red played a Jab and Blue played a Retreat, Red would resolve their Jab first since it has a timing of 1 and Retreat has a timing of 2.

p.2, Action Cards column (Fight-phase continuation, set below the REST entry):
When you are done comparing and resolving the first action cards, DO NOT discard them yet. Leave them in place and flip the second action cards face up. Then, compare and resolve the second action cards just like you did with the first action cards.

p.2, 3) DISCARD:
After you have compared and resolved both the first and second sets of action cards, place your two action cards played this round into your discard pile, and then begin a new round. DO NOT DISCARD ANY CARDS UNTIL THIS PHASE.

Derived (p.1): "Higher timing" resolves first, and the worked example fixes the direction unambiguously against the plain reading of the word — Jab (timing 1) resolves before Retreat (timing 2). The lower printed number acts first; "higher timing" means higher priority/earlier, not a larger numeral.

Derived (p.1, p.2): Round sequence is exactly: both players secretly commit two ordered actions → reveal and fully resolve the first-action pair → reveal and fully resolve the second-action pair → both players discard the two played cards → new round.

Derived (p.1): Both action cards are committed before any information is revealed, so the second action is chosen blind to the first exchange's outcome.

Derived (p.2): The first action pair is resolved to completion before the second pair is even flipped, so a second-action card cannot pre-empt or interrupt a first-action card.

Derived (p.2): Cards played this round stay in play (face up, in front of the boxer) for the whole Fight phase and only reach the discard pile in the Discard phase. This matters for Rest, which reclaims the discard pile — a Rest played this round cannot reclaim this round's own two played cards.

Derived (p.1): A player's hand shrinks by two cards each round and is only replenished by playing Rest, so hand exhaustion is the pressure that forces Rest into the rotation.

Derived (p.1): There is no hidden-information asymmetry about card identity — both players hold the same set of card types and discard piles are open — so the only concealed information is which two cards the opponent committed this round and in which order.
