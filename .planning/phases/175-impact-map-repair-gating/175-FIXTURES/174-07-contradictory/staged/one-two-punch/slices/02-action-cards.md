# Action Cards (p.2)

> Italic sentences in the source are flavor asides set inline with the rules text. They are kept
> inside the verbatim quotes below because they sit inside the same paragraph as the rule.

p.2, Action Cards — JAB:
JAB (TIMING: 1) Quickly strike your opponent!

If both boxers are in their corners: Do nothing. You can't reach your opponent!

If not: Flip one of your opponent's Guard cards from ready to exhausted. If all their Guard cards are already exhausted, do nothing. A Jab cannot break a Guard card (see Punch).

p.2, Action Cards — RETREAT:
RETREAT (TIMING: 2) Backpedal into your corner.

If you are already in your corner: Do nothing. You're already against the ropes!

If you are in the center ring: Move your boxer to your corner.

p.2, Action Cards — ADVANCE:
ADVANCE (TIMING: 3) Go on the offensive!

If you are already in the center ring and/or your opponent also plays an Advance card for the same action: Do nothing. You're just butting heads!

If not: Move your boxer to the center ring. If your opponent is in the center ring, also push them into their corner. For example, Blue is in their corner and Red is in the center ring. Blue moves into the center ring, and Red is pushed back into their own corner.

p.2, Action Cards — BLOCK:
BLOCK (TIMING: 4) Cover up to block the incoming punch!

If your opponent plays a Punch card for the same action: Ignore any effect their Punch has on you (see Punch). Block actions do not block Jabs.

If not: Do nothing.

p.2, Action Cards — PUNCH:
PUNCH (TIMING: 5) Hit 'em where it hurts!

If you are in your corner: Flip one of your Guard cards from ready to exhausted, then resolve your Punch. If you do not have any ready Guard cards, do nothing. You're up against the ropes!

OR

If you are in the center ring: Resolve your Punch without flipping your Guard card. You've got 'em on the ropes!

p.2, Action Cards — PUNCH, resolution bullets:
- If your Punch is blocked (see Block) or both you and your opponent are in your respective corners: Your Punch has no effect on your opponent; however, you must still exhaust your own guard card if you are in your corner.

- If your Punch is not blocked and your opponent has at least one exhausted Guard card: Break one of your opponent's exhausted Guard cards by removing it from the game and returning it to the box. You are one step closer to a knockout!

- If your Punch is not blocked and your opponent does not have any exhausted Guard cards: Instead of breaking their Guard, flip one of your opponent's Guard cards from ready to exhausted.

- If both you and your opponent play a Punch for the same action while your player is in the center ring: The player punching from their corner must finish exhausting their Guard card (if able) before either player may resolve their Punch. For example, if Blue is in their corner and Red is in the center and they both Punch, Blue must first exhaust a Guard before players Punch one another.

p.2, Action Cards — REST:
REST (TIMING: 6) Take a breather!

Reclaim your discards: Take all the action cards in your discard pile and add them to your hand. Do not take any of the action cards currently in play

If any of your Guard cards are EXHAUSTED: Flip one Guard card back to ready. This is in addition to reclaiming your discards.

Derived (p.2): The six action types carry fixed timings — Jab 1, Retreat 2, Advance 3, Block 4, Punch 5, Rest 6. Read against the p.1 rule that "the player with the higher timing resolves first" as demonstrated by Jab-before-Retreat, resolution order runs Jab → Retreat → Advance → Block → Punch → Rest.

Derived (p.2): Because timing is a property of the card type, two players playing the same action always tie, and by the p.1 tie rule resolve simultaneously.

Derived (p.2): Guard cards have three states, not two — ready, exhausted, and broken (removed from the game and returned to the box). Only Punch produces the broken state, and only against an already-exhausted Guard.

Derived (p.2): Jab, and the "opponent has no exhausted Guards" branch of Punch, both do the same thing: flip one opponent Guard ready → exhausted. Breaking requires a Punch landing while the opponent already has an exhausted Guard, so at least two connecting attacks are needed per Guard broken, and at least six per knockout.

Derived (p.2): Jab does nothing when both boxers are in their corners, so a Jab only connects when at least one boxer is in the center ring.

Derived (p.2): Punch is legal from either zone, but costs the attacker one ready Guard when thrown from their own corner and is free from the center ring.

Derived (p.2): A Punch thrown from your corner with zero ready Guards does nothing at all — the self-exhaust is a cost that must be payable, so a player with all three Guards exhausted cannot Punch from their own corner.

Derived (p.2): The self-exhaust cost is paid even when the Punch has no effect — a blocked Punch, or a corner-vs-corner Punch, still exhausts the attacker's own Guard if the attacker is in their corner.

Derived (p.2): Block stops Punch only. It does not stop Jab, and it applies only when the opposing Punch occupies the same action slot (first vs first, or second vs second).

Derived (p.2): Block does not prevent the Punch's self-cost — blocking removes the effect on the defender, not the attacker's own Guard exhaustion.

Derived (p.2): Advance is self-cancelling in the mirror: if both players Advance for the same action, neither moves.

Derived (p.2): Advance can move two boxers with one card — the advancing player enters the center ring and simultaneously pushes an occupying opponent back into that opponent's corner.

Derived (p.2): Both boxers can never occupy the center ring at once, so the two boxers have exactly three legal position states: both in their own corners, blue in the center, or red in the center.

Derived (p.2): Retreat is the only way to leave the center ring under your own power; it is a no-op when already in your corner and can never be harmful.

Derived (p.2): Rest is the only card that restores hand size and the only card that flips an exhausted Guard back to ready. It cannot restore a broken Guard.

Derived (p.2): Rest explicitly excludes action cards currently in play, so the Rest card itself and the other card played alongside it that round are not reclaimed by that Rest — they reach the discard pile in the Discard phase and are only available to a later Rest.

Derived (p.2): Rest's Guard recovery is conditional on having at least one exhausted Guard, recovers exactly one, and happens in addition to — not instead of — reclaiming the discard pile.

Derived (p.2): Rest has the lowest priority (timing 6), so it always resolves after any different opposing action in the same slot. A Guard recovered by Rest is therefore recovered after that action's damage has already landed.

Derived (p.2): The simultaneous-Punch bullet fixes the cost/effect interleave: when both players Punch and one is in their corner, the corner player's self-exhaust is fully applied before either Punch resolves, which makes that freshly exhausted Guard a legal break target for the center player's Punch in the same exchange.

Visual (p.2): Each of the six entries is headed by a small red icon plus the card name in bold red all-caps with "(TIMING: N)", followed by an italic red tagline. Icons differ per action — a bolt/impact chevron for JAB, a leftward arrow for RETREAT, a solid triangle/arrowhead for ADVANCE, an X for BLOCK, a filled circle for PUNCH, a small dot for REST.

Visual (p.2): Within each entry, condition clauses ("If you are already in your corner:", "If not:") are set in black bold, the rules outcome in roman, and the flavor aside in italic — a consistent three-weight pattern reused across all six cards.

Visual (p.2): The PUNCH entry uses a two-column boxed layout for its corner/center branches with the word "OR" set vertically between them, and a downward arrow beneath leading into the shared bulleted resolution list — marking the two branches as parallel alternatives feeding one common resolution.

Visual (p.2): A large two-boxer action illustration sits at the bottom-right of the page: a dark-skinned boxer in blue gloves and red-and-white trunks landing a punch on a lighter-skinned tattooed boxer in red gloves and blue trunks, matching the two characters named in the p.1 cover flavor.

Visual (p.2): Card faces share a flat two-tone treatment — a single accent color (red or blue) over a cream/kraft ground, with a colored spine along the left edge carrying the card name set vertically.
