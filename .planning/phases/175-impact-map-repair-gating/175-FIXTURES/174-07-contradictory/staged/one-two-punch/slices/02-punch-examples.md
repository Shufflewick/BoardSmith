# Punch Examples (p.2)

p.2, Punch Examples:
If you are punched and have one ready and two exhausted Guards, you would lose one exhausted Guard.

p.2, Punch Examples:
If instead, you have three ready and no exhausted Guards, you would simply exhaust one card, leaving you with one exhausted and two ready.

Derived (p.2): Worked example 1 — defender Guard state (READY, EXHAUSTED, EXHAUSTED) plus one unblocked incoming Punch resolves to (READY, EXHAUSTED). One exhausted Guard is broken and leaves the game, so the defender's Guard count drops from 3 to 2.

Derived (p.2): Worked example 2 — defender Guard state (READY, READY, READY) plus one unblocked incoming Punch resolves to (READY, READY, EXHAUSTED). No card is removed and the Guard count stays at 3.

Derived (p.2): The two examples confirm the Punch resolution branch order — the attacker breaks an exhausted Guard when one exists, and only falls back to exhausting a ready Guard when the defender has none exhausted. The defender does not choose which branch applies.

Derived (p.2): Example 1 also shows the defender does not get to protect an exhausted Guard by having a ready one available — the presence of a ready Guard does not redirect the break.

Visual (p.2): Two before/after strips illustrate the examples, each a row of Guard cards, a right-pointing arrow, then the resulting row. Strip 1: three cards labelled READY, EXHAUSTED, EXHAUSTED → arrow → two cards labelled READY, EXHAUSTED. Strip 2: three cards labelled READY, READY, READY → arrow → three cards labelled READY, READY, EXHAUSTED.

Visual (p.2): Guard cards carry the same boxer character portrait on a cream/kraft ground with a state banner across the lower edge reading either "READY" or "EXHAUSTED". The READY and EXHAUSTED faces use visibly different banner treatments so the state reads at a glance across the table, consistent with Guards being flipped rather than swapped.
