# Tips (p.2)

> The Tips section is written as strategy advice, but four of its five bullets state or clarify
> rules that appear nowhere else in the rulebook. They are transcribed in full for that reason.

p.2, Tips:
- You should never have both rest cards in your hand at the same time. If you do, you likely accidentally picked up your current action cards while Resting. When Resting, only pick up the cards currently in your discard pile and not the two action cards from that round.

p.2, Tips:
- If you are in your corner, you need a ready Guard card for a Punch to work; however, you may still choose to play a Punch card but it won't have any effect. This would not force you to break your own Guard card.

p.2, Tips:
- The state of the game may change in the middle of a round. For example, if your opponent Jabs and exhausts your last ready Guard just as you are attempting to Punch from your corner, you will no longer have a Guard card to exhaust.

p.2, Tips:
- In the plan phase, you may place any action cards, regardless of their timings, in any order. The timing on the cards does not matter in this phase. For example, you can play a Rest (timing 6) as your first action and a Jab (timing 1) as your second action.

p.2, Tips:
- In the fight phase, the first set of action cards are always completely resolved before the second set of action cards. For example, a Block from the first action will not stop a Punch from the second action.

Derived (p.2): Each player owns exactly two Rest cards — bullet 1 refers to "both rest cards", and setup step 4b on p.1 discards "one of your Rest cards".

Derived (p.2): Bullet 1 is a correctness check on Rest: holding both Rest cards simultaneously is a symptom of having illegally reclaimed the current round's in-play cards. It restates the Rest exclusion as a self-audit rule.

Derived (p.2): Bullet 2 confirms an unpayable Punch is legal to play but wholly inert — it is not an illegal play, and it never forces the player to break (remove) their own Guard as a substitute cost.

Derived (p.2): Bullet 3 establishes that a Punch's cost and legality are evaluated at the moment the Punch resolves, not when it was planned or revealed. An earlier-timing action in the same slot can strip the resources a later-timing action needed.

Derived (p.2): Bullet 3 combined with the timing ladder means Jab (timing 1) always resolves before Punch (timing 5) in the same slot, so a Jab can pre-emptively exhaust the corner-punching opponent's last ready Guard and thereby cancel their Punch entirely.

Derived (p.2): Bullet 4 makes card timing purely a resolution-order property, not a placement constraint — the two planned cards may be in any order regardless of their printed timings.

Derived (p.2): Bullet 5 confirms the two action slots are fully sequential and independent — no card resolved in the first slot carries protection or effect into the second slot. Block in particular is slot-scoped.

Visual (p.2): The Tips section is set as a bulleted list in the right-hand column with round bullet marks, rules text in roman and each worked example in italic — the same roman/italic split used throughout the Action Cards entries.
