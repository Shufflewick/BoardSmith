## Findings (recorded per plan instruction, not silently resolved)

**1. The 177-22 measurement harness hand-supplied arithmetic specs; the product cannot — the arithmeticSpec gap is real and is now closed by a pointer, not by bit-identical replay.** `analyze.mjs`'s `ARITHMETIC_LINES` (lines 113-136) is a hand-identified table, keyed by `${slug}:${lineNumber}`, of exactly which operation and which operand magnitudes each of the corpus's three arithmetic-bearing `Derived` lines uses. No live reconciler dispatch has ever populated an `arithmeticSpec` for this exact corpus, because `arithmeticSpec` did not exist as a field until this plan's Task 1 added it. The two `seven` reconciler fixtures used to prove `reconcileSlice` reproduces `analysis-run1.json` are therefore **hand-amended, not unmodified recordings** — the `arithmeticSpec` values were derived by hand from the same `ARITHMETIC_LINES` table `analyze.mjs` already used for this corpus, so they encode the identical facts, not a fabricated scenario. **This means the product path proven here is NOT bit-identical to the measured path**: it proves the mechanical pipeline (`validateGrounding` -> composition -> `classifyDerivedLines`) reproduces the harness's numbers when given equivalent inputs, not that a live reconciler dispatch, following the amended `reconcile-facts.md` prompt, will independently produce an `arithmeticSpec` that resolves the same way. **Plan 06/08's product-path proof must account for this**: its comparison against `analysis-run1.json` is validating the mechanical half of the bar (deterministic given input), not validating that a live dispatch reliably produces a well-formed `arithmeticSpec` — that is a live-dispatch proof still owed, not closed by this plan.

**2. `validateGrounding`'s deterministic tie-break, exercised live for the first time by a real operand-resolution attempt, silently collapses two of `seven` L21's four cited "both" statements onto one underlying grounded fact — a real, mechanical property of the design, not a bug in `reconcileSlice`.** `Card numbers range from 1 to 7.`, `Cards come in 4 colors.`, and `There are 4 copies of each card.` all quote the IDENTICAL full source sentence (`"There are numbers ranging from 1-7 in 4 colors, with 4 copies of each card."`) as both `quotedFromA` and `quotedFromB`. `findMatch`'s rank-then-id tie-break (`verify-enumerate.ts`, documented at length in its own comment as the 177-20 non-determinism fix) resolves ALL THREE of those distinct reconciler statements to the SAME winning `GroundedBothFact` — the one with the lowest content-derived id, which happens to be the "4 copies" fact (magnitude 4, unit `"copies per card"`/`"copies"`). Naming `"Card numbers range from 1 to 7."` as an `arithmeticSpec` operand therefore does NOT resolve to a magnitude-7 fact as its statement text would suggest; it resolves to the SAME magnitude-4 fact as the other two. This was caught empirically (the first fixture attempt computed `4 x 4 x 4 = 64`, not `112`) and fixed by pointing `operandStatements` at `"There are 7 bonus point cards."` for the magnitude-7 slot instead — which is also, independently, exactly what `analyze.mjs`'s own magnitude-based `.find()` resolution picked for this corpus (confirmed against `analysis-run1.json`'s recorded `composed.statement`, `"7 bonus point cards multiply 4 copies per card multiply 4 copies per card..."`). **The composed fact's sha256 id is unaffected either way** — it is a deterministic function of derivedLineText + operand MAGNITUDES + operation, never of which specific fact object supplied a given magnitude, so both resolution paths (name the "range" fact vs. name the "bonus cards" fact for the 7-slot) produce the byte-identical `ccb54c72d6b176e1`. This is disclosed here because it is a real, load-bearing property of `validateGrounding`'s design that a future reconciler-prompt author or plan 08's live-dispatch proof needs to know: **naming a statement in `arithmeticSpec.operandStatements` is not a guarantee of which magnitude it resolves to when multiple "both" claims share an identical quoted sentence.**

## Issues Encountered

The first `arithmeticSpec` fixture attempt (Finding 2 above) computed `64` instead of `112` and was caught by the test itself failing loudly — not silently accepted. Root-caused via a standalone `tsx` debug script printing every grounded fact's resolved `matchedFactA`/`matchedFactB` numeric values, confirmed the tie-break collision, and fixed by retargeting the fixture's `operandStatements` to the fact that actually resolves to magnitude 7. Not a defect in `reconcileSlice`, `resolveArithmeticSpec`, or `validateGrounding` — a correct, deterministic outcome of the design that the fixture's hand-authored `arithmeticSpec` initially got wrong.
